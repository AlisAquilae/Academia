# Course Intro
Delta: where land meets ocean. Many different hazards threaten coasts and deltas. 

Deltas make up about 1% of earth surface, but
- 5.6% of global population
- 4.1% of global GDP
- Up to 10% of the total global food production


Growing vulnerability of deltas to disasters
- Since 1988, over 300,000 people living in Asian deltas have been killed by tropical typhoons
- In 2015, flooding and landslides in Irrawaddy delta killed almost 120 people, and displaced 1.6 million, severely damaging agriculture and infrastructure
- Sediment delivered by Indus river to its delta decreased by nearly 75% over 30 years
- The largest deltas in the world are sinking and shrinking due to a complex mix of climatic and human-induced environmental changes. 

In order to design a solution, we need a systemic understanding (understanding of the system at multiple scales)

# Geology of Deltas
Earth's deltas are in danger because of:
- Global Change
- Overexploitation

What is a delta?
- a dela forms where a river enters into a sea or lake. Here, the sediments carried by the river are deposited near the mouth, because of the more or less sudden reduction of the ability of the flow to carry sediments. The result is a sediment body that may have a triangular shape in plan view
- Geologically: 3D aspects of the delta sediment body are important (variation in grain size, stratigraphy, tectonic deformations). Here, the sediment bodies are also called coastal prisms
- Geomorphologically, surface characteristics and processes are of importance. A delta can be defined as "the radial complex of active and abandoned distributary channels with associated flyvial landforms and the enclosed portions of the coastal plain, which forms where a river meets the sea or lake."


Delta apex = top of the delta, near river mouth
