# Basics
Centrifugal force counteracts attraction between mass of earth and moon. But whereas centrifugal force is equal everywhere on earth, the pull towards the moon is not (because at some points there is an inclination towards the moon). Moreover, at some points you're closer to the moon, meaning that the attraction force is stronger there. This imbalance causes the tides on earth. 

Semidiurnal tides occur twice a day. This occurs because with one revolution of the earth, two 'bulges' are encountered: one closest to the moon and one farthest from the moon. 

However, earth is slightly tilted, meaning that this bulge is not always equal vis a vis the moon.

Quantifying the tide generating force on any place on earth requires the introduction of the gravitational force, i.e. the work that needs to be done against the force of attraction to move a particle of unit mass to an infinite distance from the body

Amplitudes of tides difficult to find, but frequency of tides is fixed and dependent on celestial bodies, and therefore a lot easier to know
