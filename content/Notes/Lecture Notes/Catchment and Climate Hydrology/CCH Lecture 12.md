# Models
A model is a simplified way to represent reality. 

We often take the hydrological cycle for granted. But historically, this knowledge is only a few hundred years old. Water management is thousands of years old. 

Thales from Milete one of the first to try to explain what happens in natural phenomena (instead of attributing it to religion). But still relied on mystification 

Plato: "tartarus". Where does the water flowing in the river come from? Tartarus is a large underground lake feeding all rivers. Further elaborated on by his pupil, Aristotle. 

Da Vinci: water veins transport water from the sea through the ground into the mountains. 

Big change: introduction of the empiricist method: start of quantitative hydrology. Trying to measure the world around you. 

Pierre Perrault: Pluvial theory for the Seine. Pluvial theory means that he could confirm that there is enough rain falling to feed the river. Finally corrected by Paul Frisi

Model: hypothesis of how a system works, codified in quantitative terms. Not all models are like this, but computer models are. 

Procedure: 
1. Perceptual model (describing our perception of the systen, what do we deem relevant)
2. Mathematical model (describe this in quantitative terms using equations)
3. Procedural model (get your equations working on a computer)
4. Calibration / sensitivity (identify parameters, sensitivity to changes therein)
5. Evaluation
