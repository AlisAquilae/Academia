# Meander basics and formation
Braided vs meandering
- Braiding (vlechtend) means multiple channels which diverge and converge in a complicated pattern. 
- Meandering: flowing with bends

Rivers can both braid and meander

Rivers with steep slope or high discharge which varies with time have unstable channels and show braiding. Meanwhile, gradual slope and constant relatively low discharge implies meandering.

In this lecture, we will focus on meandering.

Terminology:
- Inside of bend and outside of bend (due to overshoot and undershoot and differences in erosion or deposition)
- Sinuosity (degree of meandering): river length (following channel) / shortest path. Meandering if sinuosity > 1.5

Why do rivers meander?
- unstable straight channel. Velocity differences or disturbances, which causes differences in erosion and sedimentation. 
- Rivers tend towards a meander

In meandering rivers, bedslope is not as steep. At some point, almost no bedslope difference. River wants to follow shortest path, cut through.
