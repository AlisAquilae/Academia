# 3.1 Evapotranspiration and the earth's water and energy cycles
Strictly speaking, evaporation (verdamping) is the phase transition between the liquid and the gaseous phase. In hydrological and climate science however, the evaporation from the land surface is usually referred to as [[Evapotranspiration]] (a combination of evaporation and transpiration), to highlight the fact that water can be evaporated from different sources. 
- When water is being evaporated from the soil, open water or from rainfall that is intercepted by canopy, this is simply referred to as evaporation. 
- When the evaporating water surface is located within leave's stomata, it is referred to as transpiration to indicate the strong biophysical control over the process (although physically there is no difference between evaporation and transpiration)

>[!Info]
>In practice, evapotranspiration might also include [[Sublimation]] (direct phase transition between the solid and gaseous phase).
