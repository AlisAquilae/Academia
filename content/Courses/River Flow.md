# 🔎 Introduction
In the wake of recent catastrophic floods, such as those witnessed in Germany, Belgium and the Netherlands in July 2021, the importance of flood management has never been more pronounced. 

The course commences with a detailed examination of velocities, flow properties, and discharge in water systems. Students gain insights into the dynamic behaviour of water flow and its implications for flood management strategies. By elucidating the laws of conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, the course establishes a foundation for understanding the mechanics of steady water flow in conduits, rivers, and canals. Through rigorous analysis, students develop a clear comprehension of the underlying principles governing hydraulic systems. Emphasising hydrodynamics in rivers and streams, the course delves into advanced topics related to river flow dynamics. Students explore the intricacies of one-dimensional channel flow, deriving the St-Venant equations and mastering solution methods essential for analysing hydraulic phenomena. Simplifications of the St-Venant equations, including kinematic and diffusion wave approximation, form the basis for flood routing methods. Students learn how to model and predict flood propagation, enabling them to devise effective flood management strategies. 

>[!Warning]
>This course is a replacement for the courses [[River Flow and Morphology]] and [[Hydraulics and Hydrometry]]. During my studies I personally followed the River Flow and Morphology course, and did hydraulics and hydrometry as well as this course self-study. 
>
>As such, the way content is divided among these three courses is not intuitive. RFM is the most elaborate, and is discussed in full. RF shows a lot of overlap with RFM. Where these overlap, contents were removed from the RF course. What is left is much material from the H&H course, geared more towards river flow than closed conduit flow. Finally, for the H&H course I only discuss topics which were not covered in either RFM or RF.
