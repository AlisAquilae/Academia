# 🔎 Introduction
The course explains the mechanisms underlying flow and sediment transport processes operating on Earth, which control structures and patterns characterizing river morphology. The basic theory for extreme water levels in rivers is offered. Students apply physical concepts and approaches that are used to describe and interpret phenomena of flow, bedform morphology, channel morphology, sediment transport, and morphodynamics, which all occur at the Earth's surface. Such phenomena are being illustrated and explained both during lectures and in the Kraijenhoff van de Leur Laboratory for Water and Sediment Dynamics Research. Laboratory techniques and mathematical methods are introduced that Earth scientists commonly use to solve problems in river science and engineering. In the practical part of the course, procedural knowledge is being gained by processing and interpreting data, after introducing measurement methods.
